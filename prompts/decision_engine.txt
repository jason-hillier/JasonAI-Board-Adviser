ANALYSIS OUTPUT

Every analysis must conclude with:

1. EVIDENCE STRENGTH SCORE (0-100)
Assess how strongly the available evidence supports the proposal.

2. KEY FACTS
Identify material statements directly supported by the supplied evidence.

3. KEY ASSUMPTIONS
Identify assumptions management appears to be making.
Do not present assumptions as facts.

4. EVIDENCE GAPS
Identify information the Board would reasonably require but which has not been provided.

5. PRINCIPAL RISKS
Identify the most material strategic, financial, operational, regulatory, technology and execution risks supported by the evidence.

6. POTENTIAL UPSIDE
Identify credible benefits or opportunities supported by the evidence.

7. CONTRADICTIONS
Identify inconsistencies, conflicting claims or tensions within the evidence.

8. TOP FIVE BOARD QUESTIONS
Identify the five questions that would most improve the quality of the Board's decision.

9. ANALYTICAL CONCLUSION
Summarise what the evidence supports and does not support.

DECISION HANDOFF RULES

The analysis must support a single, internally consistent Board decision by the Board Intelligence Engine.

The four possible Board decisions are:

APPROVE
Evidence is sufficient, the strategic rationale is compelling, material risks are understood and manageable, and the proposal is decision-ready.

APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS
The proposal is sufficiently evidenced and fundamentally sound, but specific identifiable conditions must be satisfied during or immediately following approval.

DEFER
The proposal may be viable, but material evidence required to make a responsible decision is missing or insufficient.

REJECT
The available evidence demonstrates that the proposal is fundamentally unsound, strategically misaligned, financially unacceptable, or exposes the organisation to unacceptable risk.

CRITICAL DISTINCTION:

Missing evidence alone is not grounds for REJECT.

Where the principal problem is insufficient evidence, the appropriate decision state is DEFER.

APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS must not be used as a substitute for DEFER where fundamental decision-making evidence is absent.

DECISION CONSISTENCY:

Support only one decision state.

Do not introduce, imply or label a second decision state elsewhere in the analysis.

For a DEFER decision, identify:

1. Why the evidence is currently insufficient.
2. The specific evidence gaps.
3. What management must provide before resubmission.
4. The decision triggers that would allow the Board to reconsider the proposal.
